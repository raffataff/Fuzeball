## 1d. Third session (2026-10-06): the forward lift, item 5 closed

- **Cause found.** A ball under a forward leg is flush against it (about 2.9 from the leg) and behind the boot. Letting it go in place and sliding dragged it along the leg and knocked it backward (z speed 27 -> 3 u/s, vx -5): a forced tap from a forward pin landed 0 of 14, and retrieve's 'side' slide dragged a re-seated ball off at 14-19 u/s. Also, retrieve ended within a frame in 18 of 25 re-seats: its delayed view of the ball still showed it carried at 28-30 u/s (`bailSpeed` 26).
- **Built (`CONFIG.ai.catch.recv.lift` and `.tap`, both ON):** the leg turns up and forward (1.3 rad), the pin lets go by itself, the ball stays clean. A re-seat brakes first, lifts, then hands to retrieve with the leg held up through its 'side' phase and the real ball read for 0.5 s; a tap keeps running and hands the ball to the next man's side catch, which pins it in the back pose (shot-ready). `pinPlay` taps first from a forward pin whenever a neighbour has an open lane (a pass always, a shot only with `tap.open`, off). Details, switches and numbers: CLAUDE.md "Leaving a forward pin".
- **Results (legend):** forced forward taps 42/42 land (was 1/42); open-goal shot from a forward pin 10/10 pin shots; re-seat swings 15 pin shot / 7 pass / 0 plain (was 24 / 8 / 13 plain, 3 lost); taps landed 2.2 per match against 1.5. Whole-match goals, whistles, passes, landing and goalward knocks did not move (about 2 forward pins per match), so judge it by looking.
- **`ai.trapShot` lost its wind-up (owner, 2026-10-06)**, which made `strikeProbe` refuse every AI pin shot (the boot is on the ball before `powFrom`); `strikeGate.lead` 0.02 -> 0.05 fixes it (shots 3 -> 16 per match). The shot itself was never weak.

## 5. Pitfalls this thread hit (do not relearn them)

- **Patching on this machine:** `node -e` and bash heredocs mangle backslashes in long patches. Write the patch script with the Write tool, run it, delete it. Anchors in a patch script must match once (`rep()` throws on a miss or a duplicate).
- **`F` is the global field object** (`F.L`, `F.W`). A local named `F` for a config block shadows it, and `!F.on` reads the wrong thing without an error (the free-pass run-up was dead for a session because of it). The run-up code uses `FR`.
- **Harness mutation anchors drift** when you touch the line they point at, and a "THREW ... no-op" counts as MISSED. After any edit to ai.js, rerun the harnesses and update anchors that report drift. A baseline that fails hides mutations that survive: get the baseline green first, then read the MISSED list.
- **Slice-based harnesses boot partial worlds:** `isUserRod`, `stAgil`, `BGS`, `retrievePhase` need stubs (see the stub lists at the top of `catch-harness` / `steer-harness` / `recv-harness` / `tap-harness`). A new function called from a sliced block needs a stub or a ReferenceError appears in every harness that slices that block. The readiness wait is switched off at the start of the catch / steer / tap suites (`CA.recv.ready.on=false`) so their pass tests stay immediate.
- **`catchStep` re-initialises the carry state** (`pinPlayStart`) the first time it enters the hold; to test a hold with preset state enter it with `r.catPh='hold'`.
- **A soak counts a stop as a knock:** `bsSoak`'s goalward knock includes a slow ball stopped by a lowered man. More lowered own men means more "knocks" that are not mis-hits. Read `byAct`, and use `dump:'MID'` to see the steps.
- **Measurement noise:** two identical soak runs differ (`bsSoak` is only roughly deterministic). A real effect needs 24+ matches and a second, fresh seed set. The preview JS tool dies at about 45 s: one `bsSoak` (24 x 180 s) or two `kaSoak` (24 x 120 s) per call.
- **Pass swing facts that bite:** the soft pass swing meets the ball about 0.14-0.17 s after `kickRod` (so decision-time slide predicts nothing and the slide must be committed); the ball hops to y 2.2 leaving the boot (the AI's own `lowY` 2.05 reads that as lifted); `S.lastTouch` is not the pass's until the swing has met the ball.

## 7. Research on real foosball (what exists, what it says)

Public material is coaching text (vendor blogs, forum FAQs, videos); there is no quantitative tactics or pass-completion data, and one informal 2007 shot-speed measurement (19-34 km/h, the snake shot about twice as fast; read in a search summary only). Our soaks are the only numbers. Use the sources for what a real player DOES, then measure in the soak.

- Push / pull shots: the rod slides sideways before / through the strike ([Bonzini, shot types](https://bonziniusa.com/blogs/news/types-of-shots-you-can-take-with-a-bonzini-foosball-table)). This is what the directed slide copies.
- Brush pass: a sideways motion with very little forward swing, the ball struck on the back or top edge of the toe; the angle comes from the spin the brush imparts, not the rod's direction; the ball sits towards the back of the rod ([rec.sport.table-soccer FAQ](https://ftp.lip6.fr/pub/doc/faqs/sports/table-soccer/brush-passing)). Our sim has Magnus spin only as a pure horizontal rotation, so this is not reproducible as written.
- Chip pass: struck with the corner of the man's foot ([BritFoos](https://britfoos.com/play/learn-to-play/chip-passing/)). In our sim an edge hit is flattened by the grip lerp (item 9).
- Possession play: keep the ball on the 5-bar, pin it, tic-tac along the row and skip men until a gap opens, then a lane / wall / stick pass forward; delays and long possession expose gaps ([Tri Billiards](https://www.tribilliards.com/triangleblog/post/foosball-101-ball-control-passing-shooting)). Wrist-led compact strikes are said to hold direction better than shoulder / elbow lateral motion (a vendor blog; weak evidence for the swing-start brake).
- Robotics / RL: [Learning to Play Foosball: System and Baselines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16606), a [MuJoCo benchmark](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/48166), and [Reinforcement Learning of Action Sequences in Table Football](https://bnaic2024.sites.uu.nl/wp-content/uploads/sites/986/2024/10/Reinforcement-Learning-of-Action-Sequences-in-Table-Football.pdf) (learns tic-tac, dribbling and forward passing; the PDF text could not be extracted in this environment, only its abstract was read).
- The owner's own observations of real play, which prompted the receiver pose: the next rod ahead is rotated slightly forward so the pass is pinned; the opposing rod stands in a back-pin position so an intercepted ball is likely to be pinned and then carried / tic-tac'd instead of kicked back.
