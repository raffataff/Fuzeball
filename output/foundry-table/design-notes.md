# Fuzeball Foundry

A physical table concept with a laminated birch cabinet, orange sheet-steel side aprons and two welded rectangular-tube trestles. The trestles bolt to the cabinet and connect through a low stretcher. Four threaded feet level the deck.

## Prototype assembly

- Use 24 mm birch plywood for the rail and end panels and an 18 mm laminate-faced deck supported by crossmembers. The folded side aprons cover the rail faces.
- Pair the rod bushings across the table using a shared drilling jig. Set their diameter after selecting the actual bearings and rods. The assembled view models a 16 mm shaft as a starting point.
- Follow the game’s 1–2–3–5–5–3–2–1 row order, with player centres spaced 240 mm on two-player rods, 185 mm on three-player rods and 119 mm on five-player rods. Match the plan for handle sides.
- Start with symmetric travel stops at ±110 mm for keepers, ±180 mm for defenders, ±115 mm for forwards and ±62 mm for midfielders. Check figure sweep, foot clearance, handle clearance at full inward travel, and capped tip projection at full outward travel on a rod mock-up before drilling the full cabinet.
- Fit removable shallow corner ramps outside the keeper sweep so balls roll off the end wings and back within reach. Trial their size on the prototype with the 38 mm ball.
- Use bolted steel mounting plates at the stand and confirm the assembled stand resists side-to-side racking before playtesting.

The assembled view and dimensioned layout are geometry-driven. The two generated images illustrate the finish and goal treatment. The generated cabinet view shows an extra bearing, so use the layout to locate the eight axes. Joint sizing, rod hardware and clearances still need prototype validation before issuing a cut list.
